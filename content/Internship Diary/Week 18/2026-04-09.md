# Video stabilization
- There are four types,

> [!note] Optical Image stabilization (Hardware)
> - Use gyroscope to align the camera lens.
> - **Pros**
> 	- Effective for a wide range of frequencies of movement.
> 	- No loss in image quality.
> - **Cons**
> 	- Adds weight and cost to lenses and cameras.
> 	- Limited effectiveness against very large movements.

> [!note] Digital Image Stabilization (Software)
> - Post process the video footage for any jitters or shakes then digitally crops and adjusts the image in an attempt to reduce these movements.
> - Can be found in consumer-grade cameras.
> - **Pros**
> 	- Doesn't require specialized hardware.
> 	- Can be used in conjunction with other stabilization methods.
> - **Cons**
> 	- Can reduce image resolution.
> 	- Less effective in low light.

> [!note] Electronic Image Stabilization (Hardware + Software)
> - Similar to DIS but takes things step further by using sensors within the camera body to capture additional information about the movement of the camera.
> - This data is then used to make more precise adjustments to the footage, resulting in smoother and more stable images.
> - EIS is found in action cameras and drones.
> - **Pros**
> 	- Effective for large movements and quick changes in direction.
> 	- Can be used in challenging environments. (High wind)
> - **Cons**
> 	- May result in reduced image quality.
> 	- Can be more expensive than DIS.

> [!note] Gimbal stabilization (Mechanical)
> - Use motorized gimbals to hold the camera steady during filming.
> - Gimbal stabilizatin physically isolates the camera from any external movement.
> - **Pros**
> 	- Offers the highest level of stability.
> 	- Can be used in challenging environments.
> - **Cons**
> 	- Requires additional equipment and setup time.
> 	- More expensive than other stabilization methods.
